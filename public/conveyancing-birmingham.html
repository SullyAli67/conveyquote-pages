Understanding costs

What affects conveyancing fees for a Birmingham property?

Conveyancing fees are made up of two parts: the solicitor's legal fee and disbursements. Disbursements are third-party costs – Land Registry fees, local authority searches, drainage searches – that the solicitor pays on your behalf and passes on to you. Both should be clear from the outset. Here is what typically affects the total.

Factor What it means for your fees
Property price The higher the price, the higher the Land Registry fee. Solicitors may also scale their legal fee slightly on higher-value properties due to increased professional risk.
Leasehold vs freehold Common in Birmingham Leasehold transactions require significantly more legal work – reviewing the lease, raising enquiries with the management company or freeholder, and checking service charge accounts. Expect a higher fee for leasehold.
Mortgage or cash If you are buying with a mortgage, your solicitor also acts for your lender – which adds legal work. Cash buyers generally pay slightly less. Remortgages are priced separately and are typically more straightforward.
Gifted deposit If part of your deposit is a gift from a parent or relative, your solicitor must verify the source of funds for your lender. This normally carries a modest additional fee.
Shared ownership Common in Birmingham Buying a shared ownership property involves a more complex legal structure and a separate lease to review. Not all solicitors handle these – your ConveyQuote match will have appropriate experience.
New build Common in Birmingham Developer exchange deadlines, collateral warranties, and housebuilder contract requirements all add to the workload and the fee. This will be clearly disclosed on your ConveyQuote quote.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions about conveyancing in Birmingham

How much does conveyancing cost in Birmingham?

For a standard freehold purchase in Birmingham, total conveyancing costs – including legal fees, searches, and Land Registry fees – typically range from £1,200 to £2,000. Leasehold properties run higher due to the additional legal work, usually from £1,500 upwards. New build and shared ownership transactions are priced separately. Request a reviewed quote through ConveyQuote for an accurate figure tailored to your property and situation.

How long does conveyancing take in Birmingham?

Most freehold purchases or sales in Birmingham complete within ten to fourteen weeks of an offer being accepted. Leasehold transactions – especially those involving management companies – often take sixteen weeks or more. Chain transactions and new builds can extend timescales further. Twelve weeks is a realistic expectation for a straightforward case, though your solicitor will advise once they have the details.

Do I need a Birmingham-based solicitor?

No. Conveyancing in England and Wales is conducted almost entirely by email, post, and secure digital portals. There is rarely any need to visit a physical office. Solicitors on the ConveyQuote panel are regulated across England and Wales and can handle your Birmingham property transaction efficiently from anywhere in the country.

What types of property transactions does ConveyQuote cover in Birmingham?

ConveyQuote covers all standard residential transactions in Birmingham: freehold and leasehold purchases, property sales, remortgages, new build purchases, shared ownership, Right to Buy, and buy-to-let acquisitions. Each enquiry is reviewed before a quote is issued to ensure the pricing reflects the actual complexity of your transaction.

What disbursements will I pay on a Birmingham property?

Disbursements are third-party costs your solicitor pays on your behalf. For a Birmingham property these typically include a local authority search with Birmingham City Council, water and drainage searches, environmental and chancel searches, Land Registry registration fees, and any lender-related costs if you are buying with a mortgage. All disbursements are itemised separately on every ConveyQuote quote.
