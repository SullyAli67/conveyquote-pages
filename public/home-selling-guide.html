How long does it take?

From accepted offer to completion in roughly 12 to 16 weeks

Once you have accepted an offer, most sales in England and Wales complete within twelve to sixteen weeks — though finding a buyer in the first place can take anywhere from days to months. The biggest variables are your buyer's position, whether you are leasehold, and the length of the chain. Step by step

The seven stages of selling a home

Tap any stage to see what happens, who is responsible, how to speed it up, and the pitfalls to avoid. The single biggest lever a seller has is preparing paperwork early.

1 Prepare to sell Before you market

What happens: Get an Energy Performance Certificate (a legal requirement before marketing), choose an estate agent, agree an asking price, and gather your paperwork — title deeds, guarantees and certificates, plus lease and management details if you are leasehold.

Who's involved: You, your estate agent and an EPC assessor.

Speed it up: Gather your documents and start the protocol forms before you even accept an offer. Sellers who do this can save the whole chain weeks.

Watch out for: Missing certificates for windows, a boiler or building work cause enquiry delays later. Track them down now.

2 Accept an offer Varies

What happens: Your agent markets the property, you receive and negotiate offers, and accept one. As with buying, an accepted offer is not legally binding until exchange.

Who's involved: You and your estate agent.

Speed it up: Check the buyer's position before accepting — a first-time buyer or cash buyer with no chain is far less likely to fall through.

Watch out for: The highest offer is not always the safest. A proceedable buyer often beats a higher offer that sits at the top of a long, fragile chain.

3 Instruct your conveyancer Week 1

What happens: You instruct a conveyancer, who opens your file, runs ID checks, and sends you the protocol forms — the Property Information Form (TA6), the Fittings and Contents Form (TA10), and for leasehold the Leasehold Information Form (TA7). You complete these and provide your title documents.

Who's involved: You and your conveyancer.

Speed it up: Complete the forms thoroughly and quickly. They drive the entire buyer-side process, so a fast, accurate pack keeps everyone moving.

Watch out for: Vague or incomplete forms simply generate more enquiries later and slow the whole sale down.

4 Respond to the buyer's enquiries Weeks 2–6

What happens: The buyer's conveyancer reviews your contract pack and raises pre-contract enquiries. Your conveyancer answers them, coming back to you where needed — for example on boundaries, alterations or any disputes.

Who's involved: Both conveyancers, you, and (if leasehold) your managing agent.

Speed it up: Answer your conveyancer's questions within a day or two. If you are leasehold, chase your managing agent for the management pack — it is usually the slowest single item.

Watch out for: Leasehold packs can take weeks and carry a fee. Order yours as early as you possibly can.

5 Agree a completion date and exchange Weeks 6–10

What happens: Once enquiries are answered and the buyer's finance and searches are in place, a completion date is agreed across the chain and contracts are exchanged. The sale is now legally binding on both sides.

Who's involved: Both conveyancers and everyone else in the chain.

Speed it up: Be flexible on dates — the chain moves at the speed of its least flexible member.

Watch out for: Do not commit to removals or your onward purchase's completion until you have actually exchanged on your sale.

6 Completion and moving out + 1–2 weeks

What happens: On completion day the buyer's funds arrive, you hand over the keys (usually via the estate agent), and your conveyancer redeems any outstanding mortgage from the proceeds. You must be moved out by the agreed time.

Who's involved: Both conveyancers, you and the estate agent.

Speed it up: Be packed and ready — completion can happen earlier in the day than you expect.

Watch out for: You remain responsible for the property until the keys are released, so keep your buildings insurance running until completion.

7 After completion Days after

What happens: Your conveyancer settles your outstanding mortgage, pays the estate agent's fee from the proceeds, and sends you a completion statement with the balance due to you. The sale is done.

Who's involved: Your conveyancer.

Speed it up: Provide your bank details promptly so your funds can be sent without delay.

Watch out for: Check the completion statement so you understand every deduction before the balance reaches you.

Common questions

Selling a house: your questions answered

How long does it take to sell a house?

From accepting an offer to completion typically takes twelve to sixteen weeks, although finding a buyer first can take anywhere from days to several months depending on your market and price. Leasehold properties and long chains extend the timeline. A seller who prepares their paperwork early and responds to enquiries quickly can shave weeks off the average.

Do I need a solicitor to sell my house?

In practice, yes. Transferring legal ownership requires a conveyancer to handle the contract, the buyer's pre-contract enquiries, redeeming your mortgage and dealing with HM Land Registry. While it is not strictly illegal to do your own conveyancing, selling without a regulated professional is very rare and carries real risk if anything is mishandled.

What documents do I need to sell my house?

You will need an Energy Performance Certificate, your title documents, and completed protocol forms — the TA6 Property Information Form and TA10 Fittings and Contents Form, plus the TA7 Leasehold Information Form if your property is leasehold. Gather any guarantees and certificates for work done, such as windows, boilers, electrics or extensions, as your buyer's conveyancer will ask for them.

Can the buyer pull out after making an offer?

Yes. Until exchange of contracts, either side can withdraw without legal penalty — this is why buyer quality matters so much. Choosing a proceedable buyer, such as a first-time buyer or a cash buyer with no chain, substantially reduces the risk of a sale falling through before exchange.

How much does it cost to sell a house?

The two main costs are your estate agent's fee, commonly one to three per cent plus VAT of the sale price, and your conveyancing fee, typically around £900 to £1,500 plus VAT and disbursements. You will also need an EPC. Importantly, there is no Stamp Duty when you sell — that is a cost for your buyer.

What is the difference between exchange and completion?

Exchange of contracts is when the sale becomes legally binding and a completion date is fixed; after this point, neither side can walk away without serious financial consequences. Completion is when the buyer's money reaches your conveyancer, the keys are handed over, and you must have moved out. The two are usually one to two weeks apart.
