The agent's challenge

"Can you recommend a solicitor?" is a trap — until it isn't

Every agent knows the moment. The offer is agreed, the client beams, and then: "Can you recommend a solicitor?" You are not a law firm, so you cannot do the work yourself. Point them at a comparison site and they vanish into a funnel that markets to them forever. Recommend a local firm informally and you carry the reputational risk with no visibility and no reward — and if the conveyancing drags, it is your sale that wobbles.

Conveyancing is also the single most common reason agreed sales stall. Slow quotes, slow responses and clients who take weeks to instruct anyone all push your completion — and your sales commission — further away. The conveyancing moment matters to your business whether you earn from it or not.

Our approach

You stay the named contact. We do the plumbing.

Refer the client through your partner portal and the quote goes out with your name and your personal note attached — "Sarah at the branch asked us to prepare this for you" rather than a cold email from a stranger. The referral fee is disclosed to your client exactly as the SRA's transparency rules require, which keeps the introduction clean and keeps you on the right side of your own reputation.

From there, we do not market to your client, we do not resell their details, and we do not cross-sell them anything. They get one thing: a reviewed, itemised, fixed conveyancing quote, and — if they accept — an SRA-regulated solicitor matched to their transaction. You get something agents almost never have: live visibility of the case status, from client care letter to completion, in the same portal you referred from.

That is the honest version of "white label". Your relationship stays yours, your client knows who to thank, and the legal work happens properly in the background.

How agents earn

What the conveyancing moment is actually worth

Commission is typically £100–£300 per completed transaction, depending on transaction type — agreed with you up front, fixed per case type, no clawback surprises. Worked through a typical agency's year, it adds up quickly:

Scenario What it earns
A single agreed sale Your seller needs sale conveyancing and your buyer needs purchase conveyancing — two potential referrals from one transaction, each earning in the £150–£300 range on completion.
A moving household A client selling one property and buying another is a combined sale-and-purchase matter — a higher-value referral from a conversation you were having anyway.
The remortgage tail Buyers you refer this year are remortgage referrals in two to five years. Landlord clients refinance repeatedly. A referral book quietly becomes recurring income.
A 100-transaction year An agency completing 100 referred transactions a year at £150–£300 each is adding roughly £15,000–£30,000 of revenue — with no extra headcount and no regulatory burden. See the full maths.

Security & compliance

Built on regulation, not workarounds

Every solicitor on our panel is SRA-regulated. The legal work on every matter you refer is carried out by an independent firm regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, with the professional obligations, supervision and insurance that brings. Referral fees are disclosed to your client exactly as the SRA's transparency rules require — done properly, disclosure builds trust rather than denting it.

Your client's data is handled under UK GDPR. Details travel over encrypted connections and sit on encrypted infrastructure, and they are used for one purpose: progressing the matter you referred. Your client remains your client. We do not market to the people you refer, we do not sell or share their data beyond the solicitor handling their matter, and we do not cross-sell other services to them. We exist to handle the legal-side plumbing — the relationship you have built stays yours.
